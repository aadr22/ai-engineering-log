## Tue Nov 3: Vectors, norms, and cosine similarity

### What I learned
- A dot product multiplies matching components and sums the results
- `||v|| = sqrt(v · v)` because `v · v` is the sum of squared components
- Cosine similarity is `(a · b) / (||a|| ||b||)`: 0 for orthogonal vectors,
  1 for vectors pointing in the same direction, and -1 for opposite directions
- Expanding `(a - b) · (a - b)` gives
  `||a||² - 2(a · b) + ||b||²`, the identity used to speed up pairwise distances
- Normalizing each average digit image to unit length makes
  `M_unit @ M_unit.T` a cosine-similarity matrix

### What I built
- Plain-loop `dot(a, b)`, checked against `np.dot` (results: 24, 0, 32)
- `norm(v)` and `cosine_similarity(a, b)`, checked against NumPy
- Verified the distance identity on random 64-D vectors with `np.isclose`
- Averaged the digits dataset by class and plotted pairwise cosine similarities:
  digits 1 and 8 were the most similar different-digit pair (0.933)

### What confused me
- Average digit similarity makes the 8→1 errors plausible, but cannot explain
  the individual mistakes; each handwritten image has its own shape and variation

## Mon Nov 9: Matmul, transformations, cosine search

### Part A: Matrix multiplication, three ways
- Each output entry of A @ B is one row of A dotted with one column of B
- All three versions match on a small example and on random (50, 64) @ (64, 30)
- Timings: triple loop 46.6 ms · loop of dot() calls 22.9 ms · `@` 9.18 µs
  (~5,000x faster than the triple loop: one compiled routine instead of
  96,000 Python-level multiply-adds)
- Prediction was v3, v1, v2; actual order was v3, v2, v1
- Shape check: the assert fires with a clear message on (2, 3) @ (2, 3)

### Part B: Matrices as transformations
- The columns of a matrix show where [1, 0] and [0, 1] land
- Rotation preserved both norms and the dot product (0.320699 before and after),
  since it keeps lengths and angles and a·b = ||a|| ||b|| cos θ
- Uniform scale ×2 doubled each norm and multiplied the dot product by 4
- Shear-then-rotate ≠ rotate-then-shear: why A @ B ≠ B @ A

### Part C: Cosine similarity search over digits
- Normalize rows once; then one matrix multiply gives every cosine similarity
  (a small version of what a vector database does)
- Cosine vs L2 k-NN: k=1 gains one image (0.9889 vs 0.9861); k=3 and k=5 match
- One image (~0.28%) is noise, not evidence that cosine is better here
- A pixel vector's length roughly tracks total ink; ignoring it helps when the
  same digit is written darker or thicker, and hurts when ink amount carries meaning
